We’ve spent a lot of time looking back at the year’s biggest games: Wardogs, Wolverine, Resident Evil Requiem, and so many more. So, for a change, we thought we’d look forward at Q4 2026. As you know, it’s absolutely stacked, so let’s get into it.
We’ve also been talking a lot about well-known games and franchises, so let’s start with a lesser-known pick that’s tracking well.
## Guildrun is on track to be a modest hit among a dedicated, niche community
Guildrun, made by Munich-based indie studio Leyline, is an upcoming PvE autobattler roguelike. As someone obsessed with both of those genres, I loved the demo’s satisfying mix of strategy, synergy, and rolling with whatever build the RNG chucks at you.
It’s very replayable, there’s no queue and timer, and bloody brilliant. I’m not the only one who thinks so. About 548K folks have played Guildrun‘s demo since it launched on July 16, and the main game’s sitting on 437K Steam wishlists.
The demo’s been a hit with players
Obviously, it’s hard to tell a hit from wishlist numbers and demo CCUs, but it’s the Guildrun demo engagement and conversion that’s really putting this one on our radar. Our Steam demo data (the most robust in the industry) shows that:

Of Guildrun‘s 437K wishlisters, 55% have also played the demo, and 54% played it this month. So the wishlist is as much of a passive bookmark as usual. There’s an actively engaged audience here.

Almost 50% of Guildrun demo players put in over 5 hours, 30% played for over 10, 14% cleared 20 hours, and 4% went past 50, on a bloody demo. Average playtime is 14.9 hours, median is 6.7, and a little over 20% of the demo players bounced after an hour.

The demo pulled under 70K new players this month but over 150K players overall, meaning people are returning to a free demo weeks later. That ‘s telling, I reckon.

Guildrun’s top game by crossover is Slay the Spire 2 (42% of demo players have played it, 19% this month), so it’s fishing in the right pond. I wouldn’t make the rod cost much more than $19.99, though.
Guildrun’s soft spot is China, and it’s a familiar pain point for anyone familiar with our Slay the Spire 2 data. Guildrun demo user reviews sit at 89% positive, with basically every language in the 90s except Simplified Chinese (72%, and 7.4% of reviews) and Traditional Chinese (59%).
When we remove China reviews, the average goes past 91%. Our sentiment analysis shows Chinese players wanted builds that cut the randomness. In general, that audience tends to want broken, streak-friendly builds, and heavy RNG – while fun for other types of players – gets in the way.
Tellingly, China makes up 10% of Guildrun‘s demo players but just 6% of its wishlisters, so China players are trying it and not converting – a sign they’re not vibing with the roll-with-the-punches design.
Still, Guildrun has a fiercely dedicated demo audience, a wishlist pile that’s actively playing, and monthly overlap with the roguelike faithful. All signs point to a modest success in a niche genre. I can’t wait to get my mitts on it.
My and my girlfriend’s top-played games on the Steam this year. All roguelikes and auto-battlers. Guildrun will be a big one in this household

## Three Steam games to watch from three VERY different genres
The Launch Calendar section of our platform lets you check out upcoming buzzy Steam releases. We’ve started with wishlists and followers – viewable by month, week, and year – with a lot more to come. Here’s a peek at the monthly overview:
I spend loads of time in our release calendar checking out upcoming releases. It’s helped me spot a few upcoming hits, too
A highlight from October, November, and December:

Phantom Blade Zero (Oct 28) is sitting on 2.4M Steam wishlists and 110K followers. It’s already sold 386K copies on Steam pre-launch, for $18.5M. That’s a 16%-ish wishlist-to-pre-order conversion before the game is even out (!!!) Putting pre-orders live when that epic 11-minute State of Play hit was a smart move, and the studio (S-Game) says pre-orders “far exceeded expectations.” As we covered here, the wuxia action-RPG is ticking many of the same boxes as Black Myth: Wukong, its most obvious spiritual predecessor as the next big Chinese action game, and it’s going to be huge – albeit not as big as that – in China. It also looks rad.

Cozy Game Restoration (Nov 5) is the opposite end of the spectrum, and also another example of a demo converting. It’s a chill sim where you fix up retro game cartridges, peeling old stickers, cleaning grime, and getting that PowerWash Simulator feeling of a job well done. It’s tugging the nostalgia strings for those of us who grew up on 80s and 90s games. It has 343K wishlists and 9.5K followers, plus a Next Fest demo played by 110K people, 43% of whom wishlisted (a strong demo-to-wishlist conversion). But the demo had some issues, with a 68% positive user score as per our estimates, mostly due to poor tutorialisation. Crucially, the dev listened, delayed the game, and has another demo coming on October 1 that directly addresses the complaints. The response to that second demo is make-or-break here. It’s an early warning sign that a wishlist count or demo CCU number alone can’t give.

Warhammer 40,000: Dawn of War IV (Dec 3) won’t be on Steam till December, but it’s already sold 90K copies and made $4M pre-launch, on 1.4M wishlists and 91K followers. Last year’s remaster of the original Dawn of War sold over 750K copies, and Warhammer games have been absolutely KILLING it lately, so once the marketing campaign kicks in properly, this one could blow up bigger than people expect.

## Steam games to watch in the next two weeks
Here’s what the next couple of weeks looks like in the Launch Calendar:
We’ve got two big Xbox games and two movie IPs your dad might’ve liked:

Modern Warfare 4 (Oct 23) is doing decently on Steam pre-launch, with almost 450K copies sold and $25M in gross revenue before it’s even out. That points to the best Call of Duty launch on Steam since the franchise returned to the platform four years ago, ending Battle.net exclusivity. There’ll be no Game Pass-cannibalised copies on Steam or Xbox this time. But GTA 6 landing under a month after MW4 will likely eat into its retention, especially on console.

Gears of War: E-Day (Oct 6, or Oct 1 for early-access) has sold 120K copies on Steam for nearly $7M. It’s sold under 20K in the last six weeks despite launching this week, so the pre-launch curve is soft (but it’s increasing a bit). Gears is a far more niche IP than Call of Duty, of course. Launch-aligned, E-Day has already sold more than double what Halo: Campaign Evolved did on Steam, but it’s well behind Wardogs (which was on 567K at this point pre-launch, versus Gears‘ 120K, and roughly triple the revenue). Realistically, in this window, 1M on Steam this year for E-Day is a stretch, and most of the Xbox revenue will be eaten up by Game Pass. That said, some superfans will cough up the extra $30 to play earlier.

Star Wars: Galactic Racer (Oct 6) is a slick run-based arcade racer that should scratch a nostalgia itch and pull in some new family players. It’s the first proper Star Wars racing game since 2002’s Episode I: Racer, and it’s made by Fuse – a studio of Criterion veterans (Burnout, Need for Speed ), so the pedigree is there. Galactic Racer has almost 800K wishlists and 33K followers, yet under 50K pre-launch copies sold, so wishlist-to-pre-order conversion is running below 6%, roughly a third of Phantom Blade Zero. I’d say the price is high for a game like this on Steam ($59.99). Galactic Racer is out of the impulse-buy area (the now-1.1M-selling Star Wars Zero Company was $49.99). So console will likely have to, ahem, drive this one, and reviews and community reaction will be pivotal.

Clive Barker’s Hellraiser: Revival (Oct 8) is a first-person action-horror game from Saber, and the demo did well. While the game has sold under 50K on Steam, the demo that launched a month ago has been a winner. The demo got 130K players and a 93% positive score, with reviewers praising the atmosphere, visuals, gore (do NOT watch the trailer on your work machine), and faithfulness to the lore. Horror games, even the non-multiplayer ones, have a habit of surprising, and the horror community is clearly into this. And it’s $39.99.
So yes, while wishlists alone are a pretty bad barometer for sales, they’re a decent one for hype – and something we’ll be building on in our Launch Calendar in the coming months. When you use this data together with our demo data, pre-launch data, and player overlap data, the picture becomes clearer.
