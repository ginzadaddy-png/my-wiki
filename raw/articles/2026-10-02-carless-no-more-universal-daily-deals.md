## No more (universal) Daily Deals on Steam... is that good?
So, as we trailed earlier in the week, the announcement is out. Steam is changing a notable part of its promotion for its PC game store to become more algorithmic. Breaking down the shift, with a little interpretation, here’s what is going down:

Until now, the key Discounts & Events section on the Steam app/homepage is “made up of curated placements that are either scheduled directly by partners via Steamworks, or by us here on the Steam team.” (In Feb. 2025 there was a major Steam blog post re: the system expanding up to 6 Daily Deals, w/discovery wins hyped.)

Today, everyone sees the same Daily, Midweek & Weekend Deals: if your game is featured, either by being invited by Steam or pitching them, you’ll see one single large revenue spike, as your discounted game is visible as a deal Steam-wide.

The new system, launching in ‘early 2027’, will have ‘dynamic and personalized’ recommendations in this section, but no universal Daily/Weekend Deals. And Valve has already “paused scheduling curated placements” for Daily Deals - maybe the most surprising part of the announce. (Existing deals will still run.)
We spotted Valve doing tests of this change with Midweek Deals, starting a few weeks ago. And there’s also been a move since August to low-level algo-feature unlabeled discounts in that section more: Hooded Horse CEO Tim Bender expanded on this, suggesting those changes “took a source of traffic that was non-existent for niche indies, and suddenly made it the best source of traffic in the entire store.”
So, two big questions here: why is Steam doing this, and is it good? On the ‘why’, Steam says that in tests, they “were able to show 10x more games to customers in a given day in that section. As a result, we saw meaningful increases in store page visits, wishlists, and cart additions.” So, they’re saying: it’s a rising tide to lift all boats.
(The other reason, in our view, is that negotiation over deals in such a busy and tight market has become a real friction / inefficiency. Key comment from Steam’s announce: “Our goal is to let developers focus on crafting the most compelling games and deals for customers, and not spend time trying to convince someone at Valve to feature their products.”)
An example of 500k+ impressions from Discounts & Events for algo-created visibility (outside of Deals!), via Tim Bender’s post.
The end result for deals here? Sounds like Valve “will have new and updated tools to help developers better manage their discounts and sale events on Steam.” But the general idea seems to be: you’ll get ‘X Deal’ featuring in this section for interested players - in smaller #s - more times you are on sale, not just based on a one-time Daily Deal.
And during the test, we did see, for example, that fans of CRPGs were being shown smaller, but still highly-rated CRPGs, instead of ‘boomer shooter that is 80% off today!’ We think there might also be positive ‘platform satisfaction effects’* from this, as players buy more catalog games of relevance to them, vs. just ‘cheap, buy now!’.
(*Related: we’ve always thought the Switch eShop lost momentum in third-party games sales by featuring too many ‘90% off’ games of high hook but dubious quality, due to how its unit-based chart algorithm worked ‘til recently. That’s v. difficult to prove, though.)
Public reactions to the change have been guarded to positive. If you’re a larger entity who has managed to get an excess number of Daily or Weekend Deals in the past*, you might be more nervous. (*Increasingly difficult in recent years, as Valve shifted to a ticket-led system for platform interaction, and got more empirical with Deal awarding.)
But companies with a wide range of titles seem enthused by the possibilities: over at This Week In Videogames, Fellow Traveller’s Chris Wright says “The more a store is personalised and the less ‘blunt’ it is, the greater diversity of games it can support… that’s important because the amount of new games is only going up.” And Hooded Horse’s Bender thinks it’s “a wonderful trade and a big win for both players and developers.”
As for GDCo, we were surprised by the change - we’d just finished a project to track Deals Steam-wide back to 2020, shakes fist 😆. But we think it’s a positive shift that’ll lead to happier players. We expect (incremental) winners & losers on the $ side. But if you make great games, we’d be surprised if you ended up with less $ as a result.
One final note: Steam’s blog post says “more discounted games or sale events will have the chance to be visible on the homepage and elsewhere on Steam.” We’ve heard nothing so far about how publisher/third-party event features will now be prioritized in Discounts & Events. So it’ll be interesting to see what (even partial!) changes take place there…
